Cybercrime Case Study: Phishing
What the crime is:
Phishing is a type of cybercrime where attackers trick individuals into revealing sensitive information such as passwords, bank details, or OTPs.
It is usually done by pretending to be a trusted organization like a bank, email service, or government authority.
How it typically happens (step-by-step):
The attacker sends a fake email, message, or link that appears legitimate.
The message often creates urgency (e.g., “Your account will be blocked”).
The victim clicks on the link and is redirected to a fake website.
The victim enters personal details such as login credentials or banking information.
The attacker collects this data and uses it for unauthorized access or financial fraud.
Who is usually targeted:
College students and young users with less awareness.
Individuals who frequently use online banking or shopping platforms.
Employees of organizations for accessing confidential data.
Consequences:
Financial loss due to unauthorized transactions.
Identity theft and misuse of personal information.
Loss of access to personal or professional accounts.
Emotional stress and loss of trust in online systems.
